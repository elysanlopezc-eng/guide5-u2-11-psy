Questionnaires & Rating Scales

Questionnaires: Structured series of written items (open-ended or closed-ended).

Likert Scales: Rating continuum (e.g., 1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) designed to quantify subjective attitudes or feelings.

Quantitative Precision
High statistical comparability

Checklists & Interview Guides

Observation Checklists: Pre-categorized grids used to record presence, frequency, or duration of specific behaviors.

Interview Guides: Prompt sheets outlining structured, semi-structured, or unstructured questions.

Qualitative Depth & Reliability
High behavioral validity

Psych Tests & Avoiding Bias

Psychological Tests: Standardized, validated instruments measuring intelligence, personality traits, or cognitive skills.

Question Flaws to Avoid: Leading questions, double-barreled items, and jargon.

Item Rigor & Neutrality
Eliminates wording bias

Evaluating Question Wording: Common Bias Pitfalls

Flaw Type Flawed Example Question Why It Threatens Validity Improved Neutral Item
Leading Question "Don't you agree that school start times are unreasonably early?" Nudges respondent toward a specific answer. "How satisfied are you with the current school start time?"
Double-Barreled Question "Do you feel anxious during exams and sad when getting grades?" Asks two distinct questions at once; impossible to answer cleanly. Split into 2 separate items: 1) Exam anxiety, 2) Reaction to grades.
Jargon / Ambiguous Terms "How frequently do you experience acute cognitive overload?" Uses psychological jargon that participants may misunderstand. "How often do you feel overwhelmed by your schoolwork?"
Social Desirability Bias "Do you always pay full attention during every online lecture?" Pressures respondent to lie to look good. "On average, how many minutes per lecture do you find yourself distracted?"
